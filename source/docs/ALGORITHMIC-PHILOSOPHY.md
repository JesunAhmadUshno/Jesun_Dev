# Synaptic Weather

An algorithmic philosophy for the living surfaces of this site (algorithmic-art skill).

A mind is not a picture of a brain. It is weather: pressure moving through a field, signals that gather, fire and fade. The site's living surfaces do not draw neurons. They let a field of invisible forces shape thousands of short-lived traces, so the structure of a thought appears only through its effects.

The field is a sum of slow sine waves seeded from one fixed number, so every visitor sees the same climate but never the same moment. Particles are signals. Each is born at a random point, follows the field's direction, leaves a faint red trace and dies after a short life. Where the field converges, traces pile up into bright ridges; where it diverges, the canvas stays dark. Density is the only drawing tool.

When Cortex thinks, the climate changes. A pulse travels out from the centre, the field's speed rises and new signals are born near the pulse front, so a burst of thought reads as a storm crossing the surface. When Cortex rests, the field slows back to a calm drift. Nothing loops on a fixed timer; the state follows what the agent is actually doing.

Craft rules: one accent colour, the site's red, at low alpha; additive trails faded each frame, never cleared; integer-free motion so nothing snaps; a hard budget on particles and frame rate so the piece never costs the visitor's battery; a complete still frame when motion is reduced. It should look as if it took a long time to tune, because it did.
